In microgravity, fire has no up.

NASA has burned fabrics, plastics and fuel droplets on the International Space Station for two decades. PyroLunar organizes those results for the people designing a lunar base, and shows which findings still hold when oxygen rises to 30% and gravity drops to 1/6 g.

In still air without buoyancy, a candle flame can burn as a dim blue ball.
Airflow and fuel shape stretch it into other forms.

How to use PyroLunar

  1. Choose a scenarioEarth, ISS or Lunar base in the bar at the top. Every result on the site is then labeled by how far it applies there.
  2. Open a moduleDetection, Extinguishment or Prevention. Each starts with its research question and the key findings, followed by charts and the test records.
  3. Check and reuse the dataCompare any two investigations, or download the original NASA tables with their DOIs from the Data page.

Looking for something specific? Press Ctrl K to open your search buddy.

Where the data stops

Each bar is the oxygen range one investigation tested. The shaded band is the oxygen level of the scenario selected above. Bars that never reach the band mean there is no direct evidence for that condition.

    Data and sources

    Every number on this site comes from the experimental tables of NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics repository (CC0). Download the cleaned combined table, or each investigation's original file.

    How to cite

    NASA's Open Science guidance asks every user of PSI data to cite the investigation's DOI, for example: NASA PSI, Spacecraft Fire Experiment-II (SAFFIRE-II), PSI-99, doi:10.60555/2chp-m469.
